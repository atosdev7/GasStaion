# Firmware audit, 2026-11-04

Scope: everything in `firmware/Core` that is the project's own code (`board.c`, `port.c`, `ui.c`, `store.c`, `w25q.c`, `pinfilter.c`, `checksum.c`, `app.c`, `display.c`, `keyboard.c`, `delay.c`), the settings of `main.c` and the CubeMX files that matter (clock, UART, timers, RTC), and the tests. Method: reading all of it, the compiler with strict warnings, the static analyser of GCC on the portable files, and two small probes (`seq_probe`, `store_probe`) that were run to prove the two findings that are marked **proved**. Nothing was changed by the audit.

## State of the code

- **Compiler:** `board.c`, `ui.c`, `store.c`, `pinfilter.c`, `checksum.c` give **0 warnings** with `-Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic -Wshadow -Wconversion -Wsign-conversion -Wcast-qual -Wformat=2 -Wundef -Wnull-dereference -Wlogical-op -Wmissing-prototypes -Wstrict-prototypes`; `display.c`, `keyboard.c`, `delay.c`, `app.c` give none for the STM32; the GCC analyser (`-fanalyzer`) finds nothing. Only `port.c` (2 missing prototypes of interrupt handlers) and `w25q.c` (1 const cast) have warnings.
- **Tests (PC):** core 736, store 13,820, ui 243, pinfilter 94, display 2,041,354, keyboard 100,752, delay 2,025 checks: all pass.
- **Not tested on the PC:** `port.c` (the widening of the 16 bit flow counter, the hardware stop of the pump, the interrupts, the serial line), `w25q.c`, `main.c`.

## Status

- **A1 fixed (2026-11-04, user: "the board has a backup battery, it keeps running 1 to 2 minutes when the power is cut"):** the periodic saves of a fill may still be refused after 2 to 3.5 minutes (the blank sectors are used up, the proof below stands), but the **save at the power cut** now falls back to the save that may erase (`save_at_power_cut` in `board.c`): the battery gives time for an erase (400 ms at most), the pump is stopped at once at the cut, so that record is the last one of the fill. Test `test_memory_timing` (it failed before: 4,000 counts stored instead of 10,000). What is left: a **reset** that is not a power cut (the watchdog, a brown-out) in a fill longer than 3.5 minutes comes back with the progress of minute 2 to 3.5.
- **A2 fixed (2026-11-04):** independent watchdog, `port_watchdog_start()` (at the end of `app_init`) and `port_watchdog_feed()` (the first line of each pass of `app_loop`), set by its registers: LSI / 64, 1000 counts: 2.0 s at 32 kHz, 1.36 s at the fastest LSI. It stands still while a debugger halts the program. After a reset the relays are off and the fill that was running comes back as a result with reason 2.
- **A4 fixed (2026-11-04):** the board keeps the last request frame (`last_request`) and repeats the last answer only for the **same request, byte for byte** (ADDR, SEQ, command, data); the same SEQ with another command or other data is carried out. Test `test_repeat_needs_the_same_request` (4 of its checks failed on the old code: INFO answered with the POLL answer, a changed preset ignored). `PROTOCOL.md` says so, and the PC board simulator (`simulator/src/BoardSimulator.cpp`, also used by the Smart210 tests) follows the same rule. Left as it is: an identical request that comes again after a long time (a master that restarted and counts its SEQ the same way) still gets the cached answer; a time limit on the cache would close that and is a design decision.

## Findings (logic)

| No | Severity | Where | Finding | Proposed fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **High** (proved), **fixed** | `store.c`, `board.c` (`run_fill`), `port.c` | **A long fill stops saving its progress after 2 to 3.5 minutes.** While a fill runs the progress goes to flash every 2 s without erasing (`store_save_noerase`); a sector can only be erased while idle (`board_idle_work`), and that prepares one sector ahead. The probe `store_probe` (RAM flash, the ring at 4 starting places) shows **63 to 106 progress saves (126 to 212 s)**, then every save is refused until the fill ends. A power cut after that point comes back as a result "interrupted" with the amount of minute 2 to 3.5, so a fill of 10 or 20 minutes (a truck) is **under-recorded by minutes of fuel**; the tank figure, the ticket remainder and the reconciliation are off by that amount. | Prepare **two or three sectors** ahead at idle and save the progress every 10 s (a fill of 20 minutes needs 120 slots); or erase ahead during a fill when the next sector is not blank and the fill is more than a few seconds from its end. A test in `test_store.c` for "a fill of 30 minutes keeps saving". |
| A2 | **High**, **fixed** | `main.c`, `app.c` | **No watchdog** (`HAL_IWDG_MODULE_ENABLED` is off). A hang of the main loop leaves the relays as they were. The hardware stop of the pump at the target (TIM1 compare) still works, but the nozzle-on-hook stop, the no-flow end, the answers to the master and the display stop. | Independent watchdog (LSI is already on) with a timeout of 2 s (the longest wait is the 400 ms erase), fed once per pass of `app_loop`; the relays are off after a reset. |
| A3 | Medium | `board.c` (`board_set_fault`) | **Only the memory fault is ever raised.** `FAULT_FLOW_SENSOR`, `FAULT_VALVE` and `FAULT_OTHER` exist in the protocol and the display, but nothing in `app.c` or `port.c` detects them (the only call is `FAULT_MEMORY` from a failed save). A dead flow sensor shows as "no flow" after 30 s (reason 1), not as a fault. | Decide with the hardware what is detectable (a pump that runs for N seconds without a count; a valve feedback input if there is one) and raise the fault; or remove the codes that cannot be raised. |
| A4 | Medium (proved), **fixed** | `board.c` (`handle_frame`) | **The same SEQ with another command is answered with the old answer.** The board repeats the last answer when ADDR and SEQ are the same as the last frame, without looking at the command. `seq_probe`: POLL with SEQ 5, then INFO with SEQ 5: the answer is the POLL answer (0x82). After a restart of the Smart210 (its SEQ starts again) the first frame can get a wrong answer; it recovers on the next frame. | Compare the whole request (keep the last request frame, 71 bytes) and repeat the answer only when it is identical. |
| A5 | Medium | `board_config.h` | **The board address is a constant (`BOARD_ADDRESS 1`)**: five nozzle boards need five builds, and a wrong flash is a board that answers as another nozzle. | Read it from a DIP switch or a jumper, or keep it in the config record (SET_ADDRESS), with 1 as the default; at least print it in INFO (it is). |
| A6 | Medium | `main.c` (clock), `stm32f4xx_hal_msp.c` | **The clock is the internal oscillator (HSI, 16 MHz, no PLL)**: its error over temperature (about -8 % to +5.5 % on the F4) is more than the 115200 baud of the RS-485 line tolerates at the edges of the range. **The RTC runs from the LSI** (nominal 32 kHz but 17 to 47 kHz in fact; the prescalers assume 32768 Hz exactly) and it stops when the supply goes (the LSI is not in the battery domain), so the "battery-backed clock" of `hal.h` is not what the board has. The time is set by the Smart210 so it is only a clock that is right after SET_TIME. | Use the crystal (HSE is defined as 25 MHz) for the system clock if the board has it; use LSE for the RTC if it has a 32.768 kHz crystal and VBAT; else correct the comment of `hal.h` and set the time often. |
| A7 | Low | `board.c` (`finish_fill`) | A fill that ends with `REASON_PRESET` is recorded as exactly the preset, whatever the counts say (the calibration decides, and the board stops the pump at the target); so a pump that stops a little short of the target never shows as a shortfall in the result. A design decision, noted here for the bench calibration (work plan 6.6). | None; check the calibration on the bench. |
| A8 | Low | `port.c` | **The stop of the pump at the target is interrupt-driven and has no PC test** (the widening of the 16 bit counter, the `STOP_WINDOW`, the compare that is set again). The logic is pure and could be tested by moving it into a small file with the timer behind two functions. It is the part with the most safety value. | Split `port.c` (counter and stop) into `flow.c` + a thin port, and test it with a fake counter. |
| A9 | Low | `w25q.c`, `store.c` | An erase takes 30 to 400 ms inside the main loop: the answers wait and the receive ring (512 bytes, 44 ms at 115200 baud) overflows, so bytes are lost and the master asks again (documented in `PROTOCOL.md`; the repeat of a START with the same SEQ gets the cached answer). Nothing to fix; measure on the bench. | Bench measurement (work plan 6.6). |
| A10 | Low | `port.c`, `ui.c` | A long beep (the end of a fill, 400 ms; a fault, 800 ms) is **cut short by a key beep** (30 ms): `port_beep` overwrites the end time. | Keep the later of the two end times. |
| A11 | Low | `store.c` | A power cut during the **first** save of a brand-new board leaves a half record in a region that is not blank: `store_load` then reports damage (fault 3) with `STORE_AUTOFORMAT` 0. | Treat a region without a single good record whose only non-blank slots are in the first sector as blank, or format it on the first start. |

## Findings (code quality)

| No | Where | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | `board.c` | `execute()` is one switch of about 230 lines (every command inline). Split into one function for each command; it makes A4 and A3 easier to test. |
| Q2 | `board.c`, `store.c` | `put16 / put32 / get16 / get32` are copied in both files: one `bytes.h`. |
| Q3 | `board.h`, `ui.c`, `ui.h`, `README.md` | Stale text: the comment of `board.h` has a sentence that stops half way ("A fill whose nozzle is put back after the ... ends after Params.hook_down_end_ms."); `ui.c` and `ui.h` say the refresh of the display board takes 8 to 10 ms (it is about 4 ms since the new driver); `hal.h` says "battery-backed clock" (see A6). |

| Q6 | Tests | `port.c`, `w25q.c`, `app.c` have no test (A8); the two probes of this audit (`seq_probe`, `store_probe`) are good starting points for `test_core.c` and `test_store.c` once the fixes are made. |
